## 🚀 What whalefeed Does

The Bitcoin network processes hundreds of thousands of transactions every day. Most are small. A few are enormous. When a single transfer is worth millions of dollars, it can hint at what large holders, exchanges, or institutions are preparing to do.

whalefeed connects to public blockchain data and the transaction waiting area known as the mempool. It filters that firehose down to the events that actually matter, then presents them in a tidy list you can scan in seconds. You do not need to understand hashes, fees, or block heights. You just see the size of the movement, the rough direction, and the time it happened.

The application is written in C++ and built specifically for Windows, which means it starts quickly and stays light on system resources. You can leave it running in the background all day without noticing it.

## 📊 Reading the Main Screen

The layout is built around a single scrolling list, with a few supporting panels.

**The feed** is the heart of the app. Each row is one transaction. Larger amounts are highlighted so your eye is drawn to them first. The newest events appear at the top by default.

**The threshold slider** controls what counts as a whale. Move it to the right and only truly enormous transfers appear. Move it to the left and you will see more, smaller movements. Most people start in the middle and adjust after a few minutes of watching.

**The summary strip** at the top shows counts for the current session: how many large transfers have been seen, the biggest one so far, and the total value that has moved through your filter.

**The market panel** sits alongside the feed and gives you the price context you need to interpret the numbers.

**The status bar** at the bottom tells you whether the app is connected, when it last refreshed, and whether it is currently catching up.
